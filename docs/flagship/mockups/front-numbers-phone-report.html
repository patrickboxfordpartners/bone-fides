Why the gate exists, and why it is hard to copy

i

The bigger number would be easy.

Showing all 9,312 would make every figure on this page larger. Some of those quotes are real and still do not say what a line on a tree would claim. The gate keeps the site to what a sentence in print states.

ii

It can be audited by a stranger.

Every displayed move is one click from its sentence. A buyer does not have to trust the graph. They can read it.

iii

The work is in the refusals, and it compounds.

A list of chef names is easy to scrape. A graph that holds back what its sources do not state, merges a kitchen’s aliases and flags two people who share a name is built move by move. New articles attach to people and kitchens already there.

Worked at. Nothing more is claimed.

A displayed move says a named person worked at a kitchen in a stated role, or moved from one to another. The site says “worked at” and “moved to”, and stops there.

Dates are the thinnest field.

Timelines and era filters see only the 720 dated moves on display, and say so when they are on. A tree or a line does not need a year.

A line needs a connected pair.

Between two kitchens outside the same connected set there is no chain on record, and Connect says so instead of guessing.

05Booking

From lineage to a table, only where verified.

The consumer loop is explore, trust, book. A booking button appears on a kitchen only when its page on Resy, OpenTable or Tock has been checked against that exact restaurant.

A link is kept only when

  • the booking page’s address names this restaurant, on a word boundary;
  • no leftover word points to a second name or another branch;
  • the page names San Francisco, or names no other city;
  • the dossier’s name is the exact name the page was verified for.

A same-named restaurant in another city fails the third check. No match, no button.

06The next market

The record already reaches the wider Bay Area.

The French Laundry in Yountville, Chez Panisse in Berkeley and Manresa in Los Gatos are in the graph today, because San Francisco’s reporting follows its cooks there. The Bay Area is the next step.

What this page does not count.

How many kitchens on record sit outside San Francisco is not given here. The record holds no location per kitchen, so that number cannot be read from it, and none is offered.
