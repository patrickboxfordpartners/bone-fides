Method

What these numbers mean, and what they don't.

Bone Fides makes claims about who cooked where. This page states exactly how each claim is counted, scored, and where the record is thin. Every figure below is measured against the live corpus, not estimated.

Where the data comes from

Public food journalism only. No reviews, no user submissions, no proprietary data.

Every record in the corpus is a move: one person, the kitchen they left, the kitchen they went to, and the sentence in an article that says so. A move enters the corpus only when an extraction pass finds it stated in the article text — never inferred, never assembled from separate facts.

Sources are Eater SF, SFGATE, and a small number of independent writers and chef-profile sites. The citation gate runs on every edge: the quoted evidence must appear verbatim in the article, and the sentence must describe a move rather than a guest appearance, a pop-up, a supplier relationship, or a recipe.

Measuring…

The lineage score

Formerly called "pedigree". Renamed because the old name implied a judgement about the kitchen. It isn't one.

This score measures documentation, not quality. It rises when more articles corroborate a kitchen's moves and when those articles come from larger outlets. It says nothing about how good the food was, how skilled the chefs were, or how the kitchen compares to another.

A kitchen with twenty well-reported alumni will outscore one with five exceptional chefs nobody wrote about. That is a property of the journalism, not of the kitchens.

How it is calculated

Lineage score score = round( 55 × outlet_weight + 25 × corroboration + 20 × depth )
TermWeightDefinition
Outlet weight55Mean citation weight across the kitchen's moves, divided by the top tier. Weights: SF Chronicle 12, SFGATE 8, Eater 6, everything else 3.
Corroboration25Distinct articles citing the kitchen, capped at five. More independent articles means more confidence the record is real.
Depth20Number of cited moves, capped at twelve.

What "worked at" does and does not mean

Most records say a person worked at a kitchen in a stated role. Fewer say they were promoted there, and very few name a mentor or say they trained under someone. The corpus distinguishes these where the article does, and says nothing where it doesn't.

This is the most important limit on the data. When the site says a kitchen's chefs trained somewhere, that is only literally true for the small share of moves whose article names a mentor. For the rest, the honest reading is worked at, not trained under.

Why kitchen names are not restaurant count

The kitchen figure counts distinct normalized names, not distinct restaurants. Names are folded for case, punctuation, accents, possessives and stop words, so "Chez TJ's" and "Chez TJ" count once. But the corpus has no venue identity table, so "Tartine" and "Tartine Bakery" count twice. Treat the number as kitchen names in the record, not a count of restaurants that exist.

Known limits

The things that would make a careful reader distrust a single record if unstated.

Dates are the weakest field

Fewer than one move in five carries any date, and dates arrive as prose ("2001–2013", "last spring", "recently"). Only dates that yield an explicit year become an era bucket, which is why the era filter covers less of the corpus than the date field does. The filter discloses this in the dossier whenever it is active.

Employment dates are not publication dates

A move's date is what the article states about when it happened — not when the article was written. An article published years after a chef's tenure can describe that tenure accurately. The corpus does not yet record article publication dates, so the two cannot be compared.

Same name, possibly two people

When one name produces two unconnected bodies of work, the dossier surfaces that as possibly two people rather than merging them. It is never resolved silently. A flagged record is a question, not an answer.

Concurrent roles and conflicting titles

A person can hold two roles at once, and two articles can describe the same role differently ("executive chef" in one, "culinary director" in another). Both records are kept. The corpus does not adjudicate between them.

Corrections

Every dossier carries a Flag or add control, and every cited move links to the article that supports it. A correction with a source is the most useful thing you can send.

Corrections are reviewed by hand before they touch the corpus. Nothing submitted through the form enters the record automatically — the citation gate is the product, and the inbox exists to protect it, not bypass it.

Tips currently arrive in a review queue, not a public log. If a correction changes a record, it does so because the underlying citation was wrong or missing.
